Read this first: the credit-hour question

FSU sorts transfer applicants into two buckets. Applicants with 60 or more credit hours or an AA degree are judged on college GPA alone. Applicants with 12 to 59 hours are judged on high school record and SAT or ACT scores, where FSU says "very few students with less than a 3.5 academic GPA will be admissible."

At the October 1 deadline you will have 54 completed hours, with 6 more in progress and your AA finishing in December. Which bucket FSU puts you in is the single biggest factor in whether a spring application works.

The good news: the Social Sciences college says a conditional admission recommendation can be made if you're enrolled only in the courses needed to finish your AA, which describes you exactly. This is a phone call worth making before you apply, not a detail to leave to chance.

If urban planning is the goal, FSU is not the school

All 167 FSU undergraduate programs were checked. FSU has no bachelor's degree in Urban and Regional Planning. Its planning department offers only a master's, a PhD, and an undergraduate minor.

So the route here would be a related major plus the planning minor, which FSU designs as a feeder into its master's program. Interdisciplinary Social Science is the best pairing, and its own requirements literally name urban and regional planning as a qualifying discipline. Environment and Society is the closest thing FSU has to a built-environment degree.

Worth saying plainly: UF's Urban Sciences and Planning bachelor's has no FSU equivalent. If that's the degree you want, UF is the stronger choice, and you already qualify for it.

The foreign language catch

Your world language requirement is satisfied for admission, which FSU meets at the high school level. But every College of Arts and Sciences major (English, Philosophy, Religion, Humanities, History, Anthropology) separately requires a foreign language through the intermediate 2220 college level to graduate. That can be up to 12 extra credit hours once you're there.

Social Sciences, Hospitality, Business, Public Safety and Anne's College majors carry no such requirement. Where a BS version exists, it usually avoids the language entirely. It's flagged on each card below.

What to do

If spring is the goal, the timeline is short.

1. Call FSU admissions this week

  • Phone: (850) 644-6200. Email: admissions@fsu.edu
  • Ask: "I'll have 54 completed hours at the October 1 spring deadline, 6 more in progress, and my AA is awarded in December. Will I be evaluated as a junior-level transfer or as a sophomore-level applicant?"
  • That single answer determines whether spring is realistic. Ask it before paying the $30 fee.
  • Also worth asking the Social Sciences mapping team about conditional admission: cosspp-admissions-mapping@fsu.edu

2. Gather what the application needs

  • Transcripts from every college, sent directly by each school. That means Santa Fe and St. Petersburg College.
  • Your official high school transcript, sent by the high school.
  • SAT, ACT or CLT scores for every test taken. Required if you're classified as a sophomore-level applicant, recommended either way. If you never tested, raise that on the phone call.
  • All AP score reports.
  • The residency form, for in-state tuition.
  • Spring applicants need transcripts with grades posted through the previous spring term, so your Spring 2026 grades are what they'll evaluate.

3. If spring doesn't work out

  • Fall 2027 has a June 1 deadline, and by then you'd have the AA in hand and priority consideration as a Florida AA graduate. It's the safer version of the same application.
  • Summer 2027 is March 1, which also comes after your AA posts.
  • Applying and being denied doesn't prevent you from applying again for a later term.

4. Worth knowing

  • Every Arts and Sciences major (English, Philosophy, Religion, History, Humanities) requires a foreign language through the intermediate level to graduate. That's not an admission barrier, but it could be up to 12 credit hours at FSU depending on placement. The Social Sciences and Hospitality majors don't carry that requirement.
  • FSU charges double tuition for credits beyond 132 hours, so transferring efficiently matters.
  • Most Social Sciences majors require a minor. International Affairs does not.
  • Unlike UF, FSU's business school is not limited enrollment. Calculus is a real requirement there, but not a competition for seats.
